# Model Boundaries

The repository contains theory, formal constructions, simulations, and
interactive demonstrations. They answer different questions.

## Research claims

Research texts propose definitions, relationships, and interpretations. Their
support must be evaluated from the arguments, citations, derivations, and
evidence provided in the relevant work.

## Computational constructions

The Python programs answer conditional questions of the form:

> Given these payoff functions, enclosure transitions, strategies, thresholds,
> and stopping conditions, what behavior follows?

Their output demonstrates consequences of encoded assumptions. Agreement
between a program and its reference JSON proves reproducibility of that
implementation, not independent truth of the assumptions.

## Interactive instruments

Browser instruments make concepts manipulable and visible. Their values are
generated by the interface's rules and user input. Unless a specific instrument
states otherwise, they are not calibrated scientific instruments and do not
collect observational data.

## Infinite language and finite computation

Models concerning infinite depth or scale are computed through finite
truncations, convergence criteria, or explicitly stated Final Frontiers. Every
result should preserve that distinction.

## Appropriate extension

A new model should document:

1. the source definition it implements;
2. variables and units;
3. enclosure boundaries;
4. transition and payoff rules;
5. excluded variables or costs;
6. stopping conditions;
7. deterministic parameters or random seeds;
8. what would count as failure of the model.
